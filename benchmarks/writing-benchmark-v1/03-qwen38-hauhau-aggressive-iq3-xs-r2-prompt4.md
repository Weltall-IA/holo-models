Mara reaches for the door before she has finished unclipping her coat, which tells me she has already decided and is only now deciding how to make it look deliberate. Her suitcase is at the threshold, one wheel catching on the rug. The handle of her bag is white-knuckled in her hand. She looks at me as if I have said something monstrous, though in truth I have only told her the truth with a little less ceremony than she preferred.

I do not stand up.

Standing would make me an obstacle. I want to be the floor she can step on without feeling it. I let my hands rest lightly on the arms of the chair, relaxed, unhurried, as if we are two guests waiting for a delayed train. The room is quiet except for the low tick of the clock and the soft pressure of rain against the window. It is a small mercy that the weather has chosen drama. I am fond of drama when it belongs to someone else.

“Leave the key where it is,” I say.
