Marco raised an eyebrow. "Now?"

"In the car."

"There is no ink in this car, Raul. And I don’t carry pens."

He pulled a folded piece of paper from his inner pocket. It wasn’t legal paper. It was a napkin from the restaurant they’d just left, stained with red wine and grease.

"Here," Raul said, holding it out. "Just your signature. It says you witnessed my... resignation."

He didn’t call a car. He walked to the station. The rain had turned to a drizzle, but his shoes were already soaked. He felt cold, not from the temperature, but from the sudden, terrifying clarity that Marco was right. He was boring. He was safe. And he had just handed over the only thing that made him interesting: his capacity for ruin.

He bought a ticket with his card. The terminal accepted it without blinking. As he waited for the train, he pulled out his phone and dialed Marco’s number. It rang once, then went to voicemail. He didn’t leave a message. He just listened to the click of the line ending, feeling the silence expand around him, vast and empty and entirely his own.
