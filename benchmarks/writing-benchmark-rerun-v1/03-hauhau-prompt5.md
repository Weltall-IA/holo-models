## Prompt 5 — Subtexto difícil

### Prompt
Um casal está tomando café da manhã. Na noite anterior, um deles descobriu que o outro o traiu, mas ainda não revelou que sabe. Escreva 1.000 palavras sem usar as palavras “traição”, “trair”, “amante”, “ciúme”, “culpa” ou equivalentes óbvios. O leitor deve perceber exatamente o que aconteceu apenas pelo subtexto. Não explique o significado das falas.

### Resposta
The light in the kitchen was thin, cutting across the linoleum floor in pale, rectangular shards. It was early, that specific hour when the world feels suspended, held in a breath before the day truly begins to demand its due. Elias sat at the head of the table, his back straight, his hands wrapped around a ceramic mug that had long since lost its warmth. Across from him, Mara stirred her coffee. The spoon hit the rim of the cup with a rhythmic, metallic clink. *Clink. Clink. Clink.* Each sound seemed to stretch the silence between them, pulling it taut like a wire strung across a canyon.

Elias took a sip. The coffee was bitter, over-extracted, a flavor he had come to dislike years ago but continued to drink out of habit. He watched Mara’s face in the steam rising from her cup. Her expression was serene, almost bored, the way it was every Tuesday morning. But today, the serenity felt like a mask pressed too hard against the skin, leaving red indentations at the edges.

"You're wearing the blue scarf," Elias said. His voice was level, devoid of the tremor that lived in his chest.

Mara looked down at her neck, tugging lightly at the fabric. "It’s cold today. The forecast said snow."

"The forecast is often wrong," he replied. He set the mug down. The saucer clattered against the plate, a sharp sound in the quiet room. "And it wasn't in the dryer yesterday. I moved it to the rack on the balcony so it wouldn’t shrink."

Mara paused, her spoon hovering mid-air. A fraction of a second passed—long enough for the air to thicken, short enough to be dismissed as a pause in thought. "I must have forgotten to bring it in. The wind picked up, I think. It felt like it was getting damp."

"The wind," Elias repeated. He picked up his fork and began to cut a piece of toast, though he hadn’t taken a bite yet. The knife scraped against the porcelain with a grinding screech. "The wind on a balcony facing north. It’s been overcast all day."

Mara set the spoon down in her cup. She twisted the band of hair that had slipped from her bun, tucking it behind her ear. Her movements were slow, deliberate. She was buying time. He could see it in the micro-movements of her shoulders, the slight tension in her jaw. She wasn’t angry. Anger would have been easier to handle. Anger would mean she was fighting something. Instead, she was calm. She was calculating.

"It’s a small thing, Elias," she said softly. "You’re noticing everything. It’s making you anxious."

"I’m not anxious," he lied. He took a bite of the toast, chewing slowly, watching her eyes track the motion of his jaw. "I’m just observing. There is a difference between anxiety and observation."

"The lavender detergent," he continued, swallowing. "You bought the lavender kind last month. I prefer the cedar. I bought a new bottle of cedar to use up before we switch."

Mara’s gaze dropped to the tablecloth. She traced a pattern in the weave with her thumbnail. "I ran out of cedar. It was an emergency purchase at the corner store. The cedar was out of stock, so I grabbed what was left on the shelf. It was just one bottle, Elias. I didn’t think you’d notice."

"I noticed," he said. "I noticed because I found the receipt in your purse this morning. It was tucked into the back pocket, behind the lip balm and the keys to the city hall library. The date on the receipt is from last Thursday. At 9:14 PM."

The air in the kitchen seemed to drop by ten degrees. The hum of the refrigerator became the loudest thing in the room. Mara looked up, her eyes wide, performing a mask of surprise that was barely convincing. "Library? I went to the library. I needed a book on urban planning for my presentation."

"You checked out three books on municipal zoning laws," Elias said. "The same three you checked out two weeks ago. And you paid in cash."

"I like paying in cash. It’s cleaner."

"Cleaner," he echoed. He leaned forward, elbows on the table, hands clasped. The shadows under his eyes were deep, carved by sleepless nights and the sudden, cold clarity of knowing. "The car, too. The mileage. I checked the dashboard before we left for work. It was higher than it should have been for a commute. You drove to the other side of town. To the district where the old warehouse district is."

Mara’s hands gripped the edge of the table. Her knuckles turned white. "I went there to pick up a package. A client sent something to the old office building. It was easier to drive there than to wait for it to be mailed to my home address."

"There is no client in that district anymore, Mara. The office was demolished last year. I helped oversee the demolition permits." He let the silence hang there, letting the weight of the fact settle into the bones of the room. "You drove to an empty lot. And you stayed for four hours."

Mara stood up. The chair scraped back against the floor, a harsh shriek that made Elias flinch, though he kept his face still. She walked to the window, looking out at the gray sky. Her reflection was ghostly in the glass, superimposed over the empty street below.

"I need to go," she said. "I have a meeting."

"You don’t have a meeting until ten," Elias said. He didn’t reach for her. He didn’t ask her to stay. He simply watched her pack her bag, her movements precise and efficient, erasing her presence from the kitchen as if she were wiping dust from a shelf.

She paused at the door, one hand on the frame. She didn’t look back. "The scarf," she said. "Take it off. You’re going to catch a cold."

Elias looked down at his neck. The wool was rough against his skin, a reminder of the touch that had lingered too long, the scent of jasmine perfume that wasn’t his own, the texture of another person’s presence woven into his daily life without his permission. He reached up and slowly pulled it off, draping it over the back of his chair.

"Thank you," he said.

Mara stepped out into the hallway. The door closed with a soft click, sealing the house in a vacuum. Elias sat alone in the silence, the coffee in his mug now stone cold. He picked up his fork again and cut another piece of toast. He ate it, one bite at a time, while the morning light continued to creep across the floor, illuminating dust motes that danced in the stagnant air. The spoon in Mara’s cup settled, leaving a circular ripple that slowly faded into stillness.
